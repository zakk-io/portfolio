    Evaluating a Quantized ECG Heartbeat Classifier for Edge Devices

    Write-up Code
    Problem
    Cloud-based ECG analysis sends sensitive heart recordings off-site to a remote server, exposing patient data outside the clinic's control.
    Solution
    An offline system that reads a heartbeat from a strip image and classifies it locally (~18 ms per image on one CPU core).
    Research
    Tested on 1,000 ECG images from the ECG5000 dataset: 92.7% correct, though rare heartbeat types were often missed due to limited examples.
    Next
    Deploy on a microcontroller, a low-cost chip that could run inside a portable ECG device without a computer or internet.

  2. Software Engineering Intern — Digital Umuganda

    Mar — May 2026

    Kigali, Rwanda

    VisionTTS: Describing Scenes Aloud in Kinyarwanda for People with Visual Impairments

    Write-up Code Demo
    Problem
    More than 158,000 Rwandans live with a visual impairment (2022 census), and leading assistive glasses (Envision, OrCam) cost $599–$4,000+ and do not support Kinyarwanda.
    Solution
    A system that describes a scene or reads printed text aloud in Kinyarwanda from a camera image, built by combining three existing open AI models.
    Research
    Chose compressed model versions to fit a small computer's memory. The working prototype took 31.5–43 s per answer in informal tests, which is far too slow for everyday use.
    Next
    Move it onto low-cost smart glasses (3D parts designed, not yet built) so it works offline, make it faster, and measure its quality with standard tests.

    Kinyarwanda Sign Language (KSL) Gesture Recognition

    Write-up Demo
    Problem
    About 70,000 Rwandans are deaf or speech-impaired, and few people around them understand Kinyarwanda Sign Language (KSL). No public KSL dataset or tool existed.
    Solution
    An offline system that recognises KSL signs from a camera and speaks them in Kinyarwanda, using a tiny 6.9 KB model small enough for a low-cost wearable.
    Research
    Recorded 36,373 examples of 10 signs myself and trained the model on hand shape alone: ~98.5% correct, likely optimistic since test and training frames came from the same recordings.
    Next
    Recognise signs that use movement or both hands, and fit the model into a wearable smart glove.
